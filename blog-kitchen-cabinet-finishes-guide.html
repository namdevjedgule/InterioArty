The Ultimate Guide to Choosing Kitchen Cabinet Finishes

August 5, 2026 8 min read InterioArty Design Team

Cabinet finish is the single decision that affects your kitchen's look, budget, and long-term maintenance more than almost anything else - and it's also the one showrooms explain the least clearly. Here's a straightforward comparison of the five finishes we work with most, without the sales pitch.

01 Laminate Finish

Laminate is a thin decorative layer bonded onto the cabinet board, available in an enormous range of colours, textures, and wood-grain patterns. It's the most budget-friendly option, reasonably scratch-resistant, and easy to clean - which is why it remains the default choice for most Pune kitchens.

The trade-off: at the very low end, laminate edges can show wear over many years, and very high-gloss laminate can show fingerprints more visibly than a matte finish.

02 Acrylic Finish

Acrylic is a high-gloss, mirror-like finish applied over MDF board, known for its vivid colour depth and premium look. It photographs beautifully and gives a distinctly modern, sleek appearance that laminate can't quite replicate.

It costs noticeably more than laminate and shows fingerprints and light scratches more readily, so it needs more frequent wiping down to stay looking sharp - something to factor in for a household with young kids.

03 Veneer Finish

Veneer is a thin slice of real wood applied over the board, giving genuine natural wood grain and texture rather than a printed imitation. It's the choice for clients who specifically want an authentic, warm, natural material look rather than a synthetic one.

Being a natural material, it needs slightly more careful maintenance - avoiding prolonged moisture exposure - and costs more than laminate, positioning it as a premium, design-led choice rather than a default.

04 PU (Polyurethane) Finish

PU is a sprayed, seamless coating that gives an extremely smooth, factory-finished look with no visible joints - often used for a rich matte or gloss colour finish on both flat and slightly curved cabinet fronts. It's durable and resists moisture well, making it a strong choice for high-use kitchens.

It sits at the premium end of the price range, generally above acrylic, given the more involved application process.

05 Membrane Finish

Membrane is a PVC film vacuum-pressed onto MDF, giving a completely seamless, joint-free look similar to PU but at a lower cost. It's particularly good at avoiding moisture seepage into board edges, which makes it a popular, practical choice for base cabinets near the sink.

The finish can be more prone to fading under prolonged direct sunlight compared to laminate or acrylic, so it's worth considering kitchen orientation before choosing it for a very sun-exposed space.

06 How to Actually Choose

If budget is the primary driver, laminate remains the most sensible default for the vast majority of kitchens. If you want a bold, glossy, photo-ready look and don't mind wiping down fingerprints regularly, acrylic delivers that. If natural material and warmth matter more than a glossy finish, veneer is worth the premium. And if you want a seamless, joint-free look with strong durability, PU or membrane (depending on budget) are the better fit.

In most of our kitchens, we actually mix finishes - laminate for the bulk of the cabinetry, with an acrylic or veneer accent on a feature section - to balance cost and visual impact.

“There's no single "best" cabinet finish - only the one that matches your budget, your maintenance tolerance, and how the light hits your kitchen.”

The Takeaway

Choosing a finish comes down to three honest questions: what's your budget, how much daily maintenance are you realistically willing to do, and what look are you actually going for. Answer those first, and the right finish usually becomes obvious.
