Vastu-Friendly Interior Design: What Actually Matters

July 12, 2026 7 min read InterioArty Design Team

A large share of our Pune clients ask about Vastu at some point during design - and reasonably so, since it matters to their families regardless of how strictly they personally follow it. Rather than treating it as either gospel or nonsense, we've found it's more useful to separate the Vastu principles that align with genuinely good design practice from the ones that don't meaningfully affect anything.

01 Kitchen Placement and Direction

Vastu traditionally recommends the kitchen in the south-east corner of the home. Independent of tradition, this often works well practically too - it tends to catch morning light without the harsher afternoon western sun, and keeping cooking areas away from the main entrance is generally good flow regardless of belief system.

Where this becomes a genuine constraint is in apartments where the kitchen location is fixed by the building's original layout - in those cases, we focus on orientation-friendly choices within the kitchen itself, like stove placement, rather than trying to relocate the entire room.

02 Where Vastu and Good Design Actually Agree

Several Vastu principles overlap with sound architectural sense: keeping walkways and doors unobstructed for good flow, maximising natural light and cross-ventilation, and avoiding heavy structural elements directly above a bed or seating area (which is both a Vastu and a basic safety consideration). These are worth following regardless of how strictly you observe Vastu otherwise.

03 Bedroom and Sleeping Direction

Sleeping with your head toward the south is a commonly cited Vastu recommendation. We accommodate this whenever the room layout reasonably allows it, since it's a simple, low-cost adjustment with no real design trade-off - even for clients who aren't strongly convinced either way, there's little reason not to honour it if it matters to their family.

04 The Myths Worth Ignoring

Some commonly repeated Vastu "rules" - like avoiding all mirrors in a bedroom entirely, or insisting every single room must fit a specific colour - go well beyond the core traditional principles and start limiting genuinely good design without a clear underlying rationale. We'd rather have an honest conversation with a client about which specific requirements matter most to their family than apply every rule uniformly regardless of the layout it's forced into.

05 Balancing Vastu With a Modern Layout

Most Pune apartments have a fixed structural shell - you can't move the main door or relocate a bathroom to a different corner of the building. In these cases, we work with what Vastu adjustments are realistically possible within furniture placement, colour, and room usage, rather than treating strict full compliance as a requirement that overrides a workable, comfortable layout.

06 How We Approach This With Clients

Early in a project, we simply ask how important Vastu is to the household, and which specific aspects matter most - direction of the main door, kitchen placement, sleeping direction, or something else. That way we're designing around what actually matters to your family, not applying a generic checklist that may not reflect your priorities at all.

“The Vastu principles worth following are usually the ones that were good design sense to begin with - light, flow, and unobstructed movement.”

The Takeaway

Vastu doesn't have to be an all-or-nothing decision, and it doesn't have to fight against good design either. The two overlap more often than people expect - the key is being clear about which specific principles matter to your family, so your designer can work with them rather than around them.
